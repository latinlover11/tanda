    Field notes

    What a tanda is

    An informal rotating savings club: a group you trust agrees a fixed share and a rhythm, and each round the whole pot goes to one member — until everyone has held it once. No bank, no interest, no contract. The same practice runs under 200+ names: cundina, susu, junta, sand, polla, sociedad, quiniela… Around 31% of Mexicans take part in one.

    Why anyone joins

    The math is blunt and fair: whoever takes the pot first gets an interest-free loan; whoever takes it last gets enforced savings — every round they hand over money they would have spent. Money in always equals money out; the club creates no return and charges none.

    Deciding the order

    Groups settle it three ways: raffle the positions, assign by who needs it most, or run a posteo — members bid the discount they're willing to accept. Biggest discount takes the pot first; the last member, who waited, collects their full pot plus every discount left on the table.

    The risk

    It runs on trust alone. Mexico's CONDUSEF advises against joining: no yield, loses ground to inflation, low liquidity, and the runner risk — the person holding the pot can simply vanish. Keep the records straight: untracked pots can look like tax evasion, and workplace tandas have cost people their jobs.

    Glossary

    Ronda — one round. Aportación — the share each member pays. La caja — the pot. El que lleva la caja — whoever keeps the money (and, too often, the one who disappears). Posteo / postores — bidding for payout position. Número — a member's place in the order.
