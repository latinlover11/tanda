Buenos Aires · the unit of a milonga

A tanda is a set: four tangos, three valses or three milongas played back to back — one partner, one conversation, about twelve minutes.

≈12min

One tanda, one partner. You leave when the cortina sounds.

30–60sec

La cortina — a curtain of non-tango music between every tanda. The floor opens, partners change.

1rule

Homogeneous: same orchestra, same years, often the same singer — so rhythm, tempo and intensity never wobble.

DJ notes

    The night

    Field notes

    Tanda

    The set itself — the atom of a milonga. Standard lengths: 4 tangos, 3–4 valses, 3 milongas, roughly 10–12 minutes. Built from one orchestra, one period, one singer, so dancers can anticipate what comes next.

    Cortina

    The “curtain”: 30–60 seconds of deliberately non-tango music after every tanda. It means the tanda is over — thank your partner, step off the ronda, and take the chance to cabeceo: catch someone’s eye across the room for the next set.

    Cierre

    The close. The last tanda is announced during the preceding cortina and traditionally lands hard — a strong tango everyone knows — so the night ends at its peak instead of fading out.

    Cabeceo & mirada

    Invitation by glance: a nod from across the room, accepted by a look held. No crossing the floor to ask, no public refusal. The dance starts before the music does.

    Ronda

    The line of dance — everyone travels counter-clockwise around the floor. The tanda keeps a couple together; the cortina returns you both to the ronda’s current.

    Hammock tanda

    A shape for four tangos: a familiar opener pulls dancers on, the middle sags softer, the last swings up again. Strong · soft · soft · strong — an arc, not a flat line.
